# Changelog

## v0.10.0 — 2026-09-22

- afpm: the survey gets a **distribution plan**, in the same file. `/design-survey` used to ask who would respond and how many were reachable, but the answer never reached the questionnaire — so `/design-interview`'s recruitment plan took its opt-ins from a survey that never said where it would go out, and `/analyze-survey` had to ask the channel after the fact, when a sample that came in through one friendly channel could no longer be corrected. The `survey-design` knowledge skill adds a **Distribution** section to the questionnaire format, after the screening + opt-in block — a channel table (channel, who it reaches and whom it over- or under-represents, approximate reach or `unknown`, one link per channel), a target n per segment that matters, a first-review date, and a close that may stay "open until target n" — and a **Distributing the survey** discipline with the same standard as the interview recruitment plan: named channels (never "social media" or "my network"), the bias of each channel stated in the table rather than discovered in the analysis (own channels over-represent the already satisfied), one link per channel so every response knows where it came from, a target per segment rather than a total (and a warning before publishing when the channels' reach can't produce it), and a first-review date always. Three new anti-patterns: a survey with no named channel, a single-own-channel survey analyzed as if it represented the segment, one link for every channel.
- afpm: `/design-survey` asks which channels the survey goes out through — one question at a time, with a recommended answer derived from the opportunity brief's segment and the personas — flags an own-channel-only plan in one line and proposes a second channel that reaches whom the first doesn't; the Distribution section is part of the questionnaire, the save step reminds the user to create one link per channel in the tool, and the close points to the first-review date. `/analyze-survey` reads the Distribution section before asking anything, computes the response rate per channel where reach and channel are available, cuts the main findings by channel when the export carries it (a strong difference between channels is a finding about the sample before it is one about the segment), reports segments that missed their target n as directional, and falls back to asking the channel for surveys written before the section existed.
- afpm: `/design-interview` (and the `interview-guides` template and quality bar) reference the survey's Distribution section as the upstream source of the opt-ins instead of repeating its channels, naming channels of their own only for what the survey doesn't cover. `/review-evidence`'s drift report flags surveys whose first-review date passed with no analysis in `product/insights/`, and surveys with no distribution plan.
- Conventions documented in `CLAUDE.md` (`surveys/` line) and the afpm README. No separate file for distribution; no invented reach or response rates — `unknown` is a valid cell, as in `/research-market`; the screening + opt-in block is untouched.

## v0.9.0 — 2026-09-21

- afpm: every persona now declares **what role it plays in front of the product** — a `type:` field right after its role, with four fixed English tokens (like `source:` values, no fifth type, no synonyms): `primary` (suffers the problem and uses the product to solve it; the design target), `secondary` (uses it with different needs or occasionally; benefits without being the target), `tertiary` (does not use it but decides, pays, approves, or is affected — the one who answers viability beliefs), and `negative` (explicitly not a target user; naming them is a scope decision, not a judgment — they look like a user and are left out on purpose). The `synthetic-personas` knowledge skill carries the table with the question each type answers best, makes type a diversity dimension (every set covers at least one primary and one negative), states that in a synthetic persona the type is a hypothesis to revise when evidence arrives, and adds two anti-patterns — an all-primary set (nobody decides, nobody is left out) and a negative persona written as a caricature instead of a scope boundary. Personas created before the field are read normally; whichever skill touches one offers to complete it. The type is never a discard criterion.
- afpm: `/generate-personas` takes the **mix explicitly** (`"2 primary, 1 secondary, 1 tertiary, 1 negative for the B2B segment"`) and honors it to the letter; without one it proposes a mix derived from the overview's `mode:` and the segment (a tertiary whenever someone other than the user decides or pays — the sponsor, the buyer), shows it in one line, and waits for confirmation. It never generates a set without a primary and a negative. The set is presented with the type in view and reviewed with two explicit questions per persona — does it belong to the product? is the type right? — with the rule written down: **discard for not belonging to the product, never for taste or "looks like a stereotype"; if every persona belongs, none is discarded.** Discards go to `product/corrections.md` with their reason.
- afpm: `/derive-personas` assigns the type **from the evidence, never by invention** — a cluster that does not use the product but decides on it is `tertiary`, one that shows up in the data outside the declared scope is `negative`, no evidence means "no evidence yet" — and the reconciliation step gains a case: the type a synthetic persona assumed does not hold in the data; the change is proposed with its evidence, the user decides, and the correction is logged.
- afpm: the type **travels down the loop.** `/interview-persona` (and `synthetic-interviews`) records it in the transcript header next to `source: synthetic`, with one rule: interviewing a negative persona is useful — they say why not, which bounds the scope — but their answers count neither for nor against a value belief of the target segment; a tertiary informs viability, not desirability; the transcript header carries that warning so `/extract-insights` reads it. `/critique-spec` (and `persona-critique`) declares the panel's type mix, critiques from the type, and warns that an all-primary panel returns a complacent reading — at least one tertiary or negative when the document has purchase, permission, or scope implications. `/frame-opportunity` (and `opportunity-framing`) lists the personas by name with their type and whether they suffer the problem, naming the missing one; the knowledge skill states that type and suffering are independent (a tertiary who does not suffer it can still answer the viability belief; a primary of the product may have nothing to do with this opportunity) and that **the filter at the opportunity level is relation to the problem, never type.**
- Conventions documented in `CLAUDE.md` (the `type:` field next to `source:` in File Conventions) and the afpm README. No new directories, no renamed persona files — all personas stay in `product/personas/`, distinguished by the field. afpb unchanged.

## v0.8.0 — 2026-09-11

- afpm: the **opportunity level** enters the loop, so the `product/` tree reads as an Opportunity Solution Tree — `overview.md` (outcome + `[product]` beliefs) → `product/opportunities/` (a problem for a segment, with signals, no solution chosen; `[opportunity: {slug}]` beliefs) → `product/ideas/` (candidate solutions, each naming its parent opportunity; `[feature: {slug}]` beliefs) → `specs/` → `exposure-plans/`. New workflow skill `/frame-opportunity`: the mirror of `/clarify-idea` confined to the problem space — one question at a time with a recommended answer, never asking what the solution looks like; walks problem → segment and personas (missing persona noted, `/generate-personas` suggested) → signals with provenance (`real` / `survey` / `secondary` / `synthetic` / `unverified` — a number in a deck is a signal, not proof) → business outcome by mode → the value and viability beliefs of the opportunity → constraints; solutions that surface are parked as *candidate ideas, not evaluated*. The brief's payoff is a **research agenda**: per belief, the cheapest instrument that resolves it (existing data → `/research-market` → `/design-survey` → `/design-interview`) and the decision it unlocks. Beliefs go to the single registry as `[opportunity: {slug}] [value|viability]`; discarded opportunities are saved with the reason. New knowledge skill `opportunity-framing` carries the criteria: opportunity vs. solution vs. outcome (the tell: a solution can be built, an opportunity can only be understood), signals vs. proof, the two beliefs of an opportunity, the agenda ordering, the brief format, and the anti-patterns.
- afpm: **the opportunity is optional, but skipping it is declared, never forgotten.** `/clarify-idea` opens the loop with one decision — which opportunity does this idea answer? — with three non-blocking outcomes: an existing brief (the idea inherits its beliefs and research agenda by reference and the loop stops re-asking what the opportunity resolved), `none (declared)` (recorded in the brief's new `opportunity:` field, with the implicit problem registered explicitly as `[feature: {slug}] [value]`), or "frame it first" (hands off to `/frame-opportunity`). Closing without a parent, it offers to save the problem + persona pair as a minimal opportunity so future ideas on the same problem have somewhere to hang. `/start-product` accepts a shapeless bet ("improve X for Y") in existing mode without pushing it toward a feature, and closes with `/frame-opportunity` for a problem-level bet or `/clarify-idea` for a decided feature.
- afpm: the research chain follows the agenda. `/research-market` derives questions from `[opportunity: {slug}]` beliefs and the brief's research agenda too, scopes the run to an opportunity when one is passed, includes opportunity beliefs in the impact table, and closes naming what goes to a survey and what to interviews. `/design-survey` and `/design-interview` read `product/opportunities/` and `product/research/` and propose learning goals from the agenda and from what secondary research left as "needs primary research". `/design-survey` adds by default a closing **screening + opt-in block** (interview disqualifiers, "would you accept a 30-minute conversation?", contact) because in this flow interviewees are recruited among respondents; `/analyze-survey` lists the opt-ins tagged by whether their answers confirm or contradict the beliefs; `/design-interview`'s recruitment plan takes those opt-ins as the primary source, contradictors before confirmers. The `survey-design` and `interview-guides` knowledge skills carry the block and the rule.
- afpm: `/review-evidence`, `/extract-insights`, and `/analyze-survey` (plus `insight-extraction` and `secondary-research`) treat the three scope tags alike when annotating beliefs; the drift report flags opportunity briefs whose research agenda nobody started and idea briefs hanging from a contradicted opportunity. afpb needs no change — `/formulate-hypothesis` already matches whatever scope tags the registry uses.
- Conventions documented in `CLAUDE.md` (third scope tag, `product/opportunities/`, the `opportunity:` field, the optional-but-declared rule) and the afpm README. `/assess-viability` stays deferred: the viability belief of an opportunity lives in `/frame-opportunity` for now.

## v0.7.0 — 2026-09-05

- The unverified beliefs in `product/overview.md` become the **single belief registry** the whole loop reads and writes — no idea brief, spec, or hypothesis keeps its own assumptions list anymore. Every belief opens with two fixed English tokens: a scope tag (`[product]`, or `[feature: {slug}]` for beliefs tied to one bet) and a risk tag from one four-value vocabulary — `[value]`, `[usability]`, `[feasibility]`, `[viability]` — with the default owner derived from the risk (value/viability → PM, usability → UX, feasibility → Tech) and written down only when the real owner differs; the list is ranked by impact × uncertainty. `/start-product` now opens by placing the product on two axes — new/existing and commercial/internal — and probes value and viability per mode: existing products separate "what we know" (facts with their evidence) from the belief frontier and say what the next bet is believed to do; internal products ask whether anyone would abandon the workaround or use it unmandated, and capture the sponsor (who funds it, what they need to see to keep funding); the overview gains a `mode:` line and the close depends on the mode (`/generate-personas` for new; `/clarify-idea` with a bet in mind; `/derive-personas` or `/design-interview` — real users exist and are reachable — without one). `/clarify-idea` and `/write-spec` reference registered beliefs and append new ones as `[feature: {slug}]`, always with approval; the `feature-specs` skill documents the four risk categories and default owners. In afpb, `/formulate-hypothesis` anchors each hypothesis to the registry belief it tests (a `Tests:` line in the doc) and, when the experiment's Outcome lands, the belief's annotation is proposed — behavior observed under real exposure counts as real evidence. `/design-interview` now closes with a recruitment plan — concretely named channels, screeners with explicit disqualifiers, and a target where saturation beats quota — and the `interview-guides` template carries the section so guides born outside the workflow get one too. Conventions documented in `CLAUDE.md` (overview fields, belief tags, `product/hypotheses/` in the tree) and both READMEs; smoke-tested end to end on the existing + internal + feature-in-mind path (`/start-product` → `/clarify-idea` → `/write-spec` → `/formulate-hypothesis` → `/review-evidence`).

## v0.6.0 — 2026-09-01

- afpm: the memory loop closes — new `/review-evidence` workflow skill, the PM's weekly sweep (named `review-evidence` because bare `/review` collides with Claude Code's built-in review command, which wins the resolution). It lists the `product/` artifacts created or modified since the last review (`git log`, or filename timestamps without git), contrasts the new evidence against each unverified belief in `product/overview.md` respecting the provenance hierarchy (`source: real` > `survey` > `secondary` > `synthetic` — synthetic evidence never confirms a belief, it only makes it promising), and proposes per-belief annotations `— confirmed/contradicted/weakened by [file] (date)` (fixed English keywords; no status = still unverified; user approves before writing; beliefs are annotated, never deleted). It also reports drift (beliefs without evidence, synthetic insights without real verification, specs without critique, hypothesis files with an empty Outcome section when `product/hypotheses/` exists), asks what the human corrected of what the AI proposed this week, and appends it to the new `product/corrections.md` (single living file, dated entries: artifact, AI proposal, human decision, why — reviews are recorded even when there were no corrections). Closes with a one-screen summary and a suggested next step.
- afpm: `/extract-insights` and `/analyze-survey` feed the loop too — after saving, both contrast their findings against the overview's beliefs and offer to annotate the affected ones with the same convention, always with the user's approval. Real and survey evidence can confirm or contradict (surveys within their sample limits); synthetic insights only signal. The `insight-extraction` knowledge skill documents the feedback step.
- Belief-status convention and `product/corrections.md` documented in the File Conventions of `CLAUDE.md` and in the afpm README (which also lists `/review-evidence`).

## v0.4.0 — 2026-08-04

- afpm: removed `/afpm-guide-me` due to circular references and restrictive linear promotion
- afpm: cognitive friction mapping — new knowledge skill `cognitive-frictions` (the Cognitive Friction Map from Alaimo Labs: four friction categories — Transformation, Limiter, Standardizer, Evaluator points — with a per-step block of category-with-why, friction, problem, evidence metric, severity, and metric impact; evidence metrics must come from data already in the repo or be marked "not yet measured", never invented) and workflow skill `/map-frictions` (resolves the journey from a file, pasted text, or a spec's journey section — or elicits it actor/goal/steps when none exists — analyzes each step through the MFC lens, ranks frictions by severity as AI-opportunity candidates, and saves to the new `product/journeys/` directory). Hands off to `/clarify-idea`, `/write-spec`, or `/interview-persona`.
- afpm: new `/test-interview-guide` workflow skill — pretest an interview guide by running it end to end against a synthetic persona, then diagnose the _guide_ from the answers (speculation, leading questions, double-barreled wording, redundancy, dead ends, missing probes, priming, coverage gaps, overrun) and revise it in place with a dated `## Pretest notes` section. The persona is the instrument, not the subject: findings never land in `product/insights/`, and the dry-run transcript is not saved by default. `interview-guides` gains the pretesting discipline (defect taxonomy + what a synthetic pretest cannot tell you); `/design-interview` now closes by pointing here instead of suggesting a manual rehearsal.
- afpm: secondary research — new knowledge skill `secondary-research` (provenance discipline: every claim labeled `verificado`/`conocimiento del modelo`; source hierarchy; lanes: competitors, alternatives/non-consumption, pricing, positioning, trends; belief-impact mapping) and workflow skill `/research-market` (derives questions from the overview's beliefs, states the method up front — web vs model knowledge, parallel subagents when available — and saves to `product/research/` with `source: secondary`, kept apart from user evidence in `insights/`). `/start-product`, `/clarify-idea`, and `/write-spec` now read or suggest it; degrades gracefully in clients without web search or subagents.
- afpm: new `/start-product` workflow skill — bootstrap `product/overview.md` from scratch: reads existing repo context as hypothesis (user confirms before it's written), interviews for the rest, and pushes for 3–5 falsifiable unverified beliefs about the users. `/generate-personas` suggests it when no product context exists.
- afpm: real-research bridge — new knowledge skills `interview-guides` (discussion-guide design: goals → beliefs at risk → non-leading questions, funnel structure, falsification notes) and `survey-design` (questionnaire craft, wording bias, scales, plus results analysis: denominators, segment cuts, open-end coding); new workflow skills `/design-interview` and `/design-survey` (turn assumptions/insights into research instruments, saved to `product/interview-guides/` and `product/surveys/`), `/analyze-survey` (results → goal-by-goal analysis + insights with `source: survey`), and `/derive-personas` (cluster patterns across real interviews/surveys into evidence-based personas, `source: derived`, reconciled against the existing synthetic set). `/extract-insights` now archives external real transcripts into `product/interviews/` with `source: real`; the README points the way from synthetic to real.
- afpm: new `/clarify-idea` workflow skill — sharpen a fuzzy feature idea through evidence-grounded questioning (one question at a time, each with a recommended answer; facts come from `product/`, decisions stay with the user, unknowns become ranked assumptions). Saves the brief to `product/ideas/{YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM}-{slug}.md` and hands off to `/write-spec` or validation interviews.
- afpm: new `feature-specs` knowledge skill (spec structure: evidence-cited problem, user journey, 3–5 critical user stories with observable acceptance criteria, falsifiable hypothesis, assumptions) and `/write-spec` workflow skill that drafts a spec from insights and personas.
- afpm + afpb: dated artifacts now use date-first filenames — `{YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM}-{slug}.md` (critiques: `{YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM}-critique-{spec-slug}.md`) — so listings sort chronologically and same-day files don't collide. Applies to interviews, insights, critiques, specs, and exposure plans; the timestamp is the creation date and revisions edit in place without renaming. Only `overview.md` and personas stay undated.
